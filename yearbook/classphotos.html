Grand Rapids, 2011 to 2014

Speaking Truth to Power

We started by giving free nights to ArtPrize artists. Just as we were about to open our Airbnb, the city tried to make short‑term rentals a misdemeanor offense. We organized, developed the Homesharers Creed and advocated: more than 1,000 people signed our petition, and we carried their names to City Hall. We won the right to share our home, and in November 2014 the Loft at Union Square received one of the city’s first homesharing licenses.

ArtPrize 2012

Bridging Humanity

We hosted Edward and Mark McComber from Cincinnati while they installed Bridging Humanity, an experiential exhibit that spanned the entire Gillett Bridge. Their curator, Sylvia, is from Greece and made us one of the best meals of our lives.

We will always remember September 28, 2012. The night the sky lanterns lifted off was the day our first grandson, Cruz, was born.

The Homesharers Creed

Homesharers for Grand Rapids, read to the Grand Rapids Planning Commission, June 2014

We believe owner‑occupied homesharing is…
Welcome it

We believe owner-occupied homesharing is an important and emerging model that should be welcomed and incentivized in Grand Rapids.

Low risk

We believe owner-occupied homesharing is a low risk activity, as evidenced by the 3.5 year spotless track record that exists in Grand Rapids.

Good for neighborhoods

We believe owner-occupied homesharing is good for our neighborhoods. It introduces new potential employers, employees and homeowners to parts of the city that they might not experience without these additional hospitality choices.

Good for our economy

We believe owner-occupied homesharing is good for our economy. Four economic impact studies have proven that Airbnb guests stay longer and spend more money in the local community.

Good for tourism

We believe owner-occupied homesharing is good for tourism and hospitality. It expands more choices by offering more spaces in more places for diverse incomes.

Good for entrepreneurs

We believe owner-occupied homesharing is good for entrepreneurialism. It provides a vehicle for citizens to earn revenue from their assets.

Good for philanthropy

We believe owner-occupied homesharing is good for philanthropy. It provides a vehicle for citizens to share revenue earned from their assets.

Inclusive and hospitable

We believe owner-occupied homesharing fills an important gap in the hospitality, rental and homeownership markets, and that over-regulating and criminalizing owner-occupied homesharing will make us one of the least inclusive and hospitable cities in our nation.
