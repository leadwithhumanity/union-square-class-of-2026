Class History

From the gym floor to home

How a tooling engineer and an organizational-development consultant turned a high school gymnasium into a home, a guesthouse, a venue and a neighborhood project, 2006 to 2026.

1900s

Union High School

Grand Rapids builds its West Side high school in brick and stone, with a gymnasium under big arched windows. Generations of West Side families, many of them immigrants and refugees, graduate here. The last class walks in 1967, and the building goes on as a middle school and community center.

2004 to 2006

A school becomes a home

Pioneer Construction converts the building into 180 condominiums and keeps what mattered: blackboards, lockers, hardwood floors, high ceilings. Brian walks through the school before construction begins and buys the only condo in the old gymnasium with the full glass window, Unit 224, three levels under 24 feet of glass. He moves in October 2006.

2007

The bird

Erica comes to see a condo at Union Square, wanting, as she wrote later, "to be part of something bigger than myself." A gray homing pigeon lands on the ledge beside her, smooths out its feathers and settles in: a bird that always finds its way home. She takes the hint and buys Unit 431, a piece of the old auditorium, and moves in May 2007.

Dec 2007

The resident handyman

Erica brings a starlight home from Puerto Vallarta and hires the building's resident handyman to hang it in 431. His name is Brian. The job starts a friendship.

Dec 31, 2008

The New Year's Eve date

Brian asks Erica to be his date for the Union Square New Year's Eve party, and they have been joined at the hip ever since. Exactly one year later they get engaged in Puerto Vallarta, marry in Northport in July 2010, honeymoon back in Puerto Vallarta, and Erica moves into the gym.

2009

Trash bags and friends

While they are dating, Brian and Erica start picking up trash on the block around Union Square and invite friends to join them. It becomes the West Side Community Clean Up, today the City Wide Clean Up, an annual spring Saturday with 200+ volunteers.

2010

ArtPrize, walls and all

Brian and Erica host ArtPrize artist Young Kim in the loft. In four days Brian, Erica and Brian's son build 12-foot walls, shelves and track lighting at the Grand Rapids Art Museum. The piece is damaged on opening day, rebuilt overnight when Kim makes the Top 10, and finishes eighth. Drywall, paint, late-night Home Depot runs, Chinese food on the museum floor. Erica, a year later: "It is the grit, not the glamour, that creates community."

2011

Ask me

Brian and Erica open the home to more ArtPrize artists. Erica and her mother, 77 and her hero, volunteer side by side as Wayfinders in vests and giant "Ask Me" buttons. On her birthday, ArtPrize's opening day, she writes it a thank-you note in The Rapidian, and a few weeks later a second essay that ends with a line she keeps: lead with grace over judgement.

2014

The first guests

In September the Loft opens on Airbnb as one of the first licensed homeshares in Grand Rapids. Honeymooners, anniversary couples, job candidates and two-city professionals arrive. Champagne for the occasions, introductions to local artists, a ride to the marathon start. From the first booking, 25 percent of the proceeds go to neighborhood nonprofits: Well House, Steepletown and HQ. See every nonprofit on the Honor Roll →

Nov 2014

Airbnb Open, San Francisco

Two months into hosting, Erica flies to the first Airbnb Open with hosts from around the world, and comes home with a notebook full of ideas about hospitality as a force for good.

2015

A new model of hospitality

Rapid Growth tells the story of the Loft's first year of homesharing. Erica serves on the Grand Rapids Planning Commission and pushes for homesharing rules that let more neighbors open their doors.

Dec 2015

A song from the loft

Wild Life Crush sings and films “I Wish I Would’ve Fished More” right here in the loft, a song for anyone who loves to fish and spend time with the ones they love. Watch the video →

Mar 2017

Say hello to 431

The auditorium condo goes on the market: "It's official... Brian and I are selling our beautiful condo at Union Square! The rooftop pool and hot tub are waiting for you to come and be our neighbor!" Brick, barn door, Tiffany-style lamp over the table, and the agate entryway, passed to the next person who needed a start downtown.

2017

The last class comes home

The Union High Class of 1967, the last to graduate from the building, holds its 50th reunion with a tour Brian and Erica organize. Erica tells School News Network: "We believe the school is a public trust that should be shared." Trip101 names the Loft one of the best stays in Grand Rapids.

2020s

Superhost

More than 1,000 guests, 681 written reviews, a 4.96 rating, an Airbnb Guest Favorite, and a rooftop pool and hot tub that show up in a lot of them. Brian serves eight years on the Union Square board, four of them as president; Erica leads the social committee for ten years and sits on the West Grand Neighborhood Organization board. The cleanup they started with ten neighbors from the building grows into the West Side Community Cleanup, more than a thousand people a year.

2026

The next chapter

Twenty years, celebrated with a weekend gathering on October 23 and 24. The city house is traded for more time at Lake Michigan and the wider world. What the estate sale earns is gifted to the organizations on the Honor Roll. One home, one family, twenty years of care. The bird, as it turns out, was a sign both ways.

The Legacy of the Starlight

Three stars, a mirror, and a wire

Erica and Brian's story is woven into Union Square and the West Side. Both were original owners. Brian walked through the school before construction began and bought the only condo in the old gymnasium that included the full glass window, Unit 224. He moved in October 2006, and Erica followed in May 2007, when she bought part of the auditorium, Unit 431.

In 2006 Erica and her mom traveled to Puerto Vallarta to celebrate Erica earning her master's degree and her mother's retirement. They fell in love with the place and began returning every year. Erica brought the first starlight home in 2007 knowing she would hang it in the condo she had just bought, and she hired the resident handyman of Union Square, Brian, to hang it in Unit 431. That started their friendship, and in December 2007 they began dating. Brian joined her on the trips to Puerto Vallarta, where they got engaged on New Year's Eve 2009 and, after a July 2010 wedding in Northport, celebrated their honeymoon. They brought back two more starlights from a small market in Tlaquepaque and paired them with an antique sun-and-moon mirror from Captain Bizarro on Leonard Street. Brian, with his engineer's skill, wired them together, a lasting tribute to their love, and the starlight has shone through their 15-year marriage.

Neither Erica nor Brian has ever lived anywhere longer than Union Square. Not only did they find one another; they built a life here and poured their energy into the West Side. Brian served on the Union Square board for eight years, four as president, and Erica led the social committee for ten. Together they started a neighborhood initiative that became the West Side Community Cleanup: ten neighbors from this building at first, now an annual event with more than a thousand participants. They spent a decade opening their home as a short-term rental, welcoming thousands of people and giving a share of the proceeds to local nonprofits serving children and families in the neighborhood. Through all seasons and for many reasons, the starlight has been a symbol of hope, love, connection and place.

When it came time to sell Unit 224, Erica and Brian pulled the starlight out of the offer, thinking they would take the lights with them. But hearing how much the buyers love it felt like confirmation that this home is meant for them, and the starlight is meant to stay, their gift to the future owners. It will give them great joy to see the lights shining from their former home as they walk past Union Square and remember how central this community has been to their lives and their love story. Their only request: if the buyers ever sell, the starlight stays, and the new owners add their story to the legacy, so the lights shine on for the next family who make Unit 224 their home.
